# MSU Libraries Service
## Strategic Plan Implementation Team ##
My MSU Libraries service has been quite varied in this time under review, via service on Space Planning Committee, two job search committees, one area that stands out is my role with the MSU Libraries Strategic Planning Implementation Team (hereafter, Implementation Team). 
I was first a member of the Implementation Team (2024) and then Chair of the Implementation Team (2025). During this time I helped lead and contributed to a number of important developments as MSUL continues to develop its practices relating to strategic planning. The artifacts I've compiled here help indicate the important collaborative work that was undertaken to refresh the Strategic Plan and further a culture of strategic planning at MSUL. 
By a culture of strategic planning, I am referring to practices relating to how organizations set goals, organize themselves to carry out those goals, measure progress towards those goals, and communicate how those goals have been met. My engagement with the work of the Strategic Plan meant was partly based in strategic planning for the makerspace and Digital Scholarship Services units.
To indicate these contributions, I have brought together the following artifacts. Each artifact will have annotation including the work of my specific contribution. 
First, a brief timeline: 
In the beginning of 2024, the Implementation Team was encouraged by Dean Neil Romanosky to create a process for refreshing the Strategic Plan. For the Summer of 2024 we planned an event for collecting input into the Strategic Plan, as it had been developed several years previously. In late 2024, we organized the remarks to the Strategic Plan. I worked closely with Nicole Smeltekop, co-Chair that year, to draft the report, receiving regular input from the rest of the team. Though a member of the larger team, I took a primary role in drafting the report. We then distilled the feedback from the MSU Libraries received via the report, and implemented changes to the Strategic Plan, leaving things in a draft state by early 2025. During this time the Federal Government changed its approach to how its relationship with the country's higher education institutions. MSUL and MSU more broadly were affected by these changes, and the Strategic Plan itself needed to be updated, and as such was delayed in being published. The Implementation Team incorporated these changes and published the updated strategic plan in Fall of 2024.

### BRASS Mentorship
Over the five mentees I have had over the past three years, I have developed a loose system that I believe supports early career professionals in responding to the unique challenges of engaging with the work of being a new business librarian professional. Both BRASS and MSUL mentorship typically have 8-12 meetings spaced over an academic year.
This systems foregrounds the Logic Model framework, which was developed by the Kellogg Foundation to help its grantees communicate how their activities build towards their desired impacts. Though limited as all frameworks inevitably are, the Logic Model encourages ways of thinking and working that are beneficial to early career librarians:
- Impact. Logic Models require mapping how day-to-day efforts build towards shared goals and desired impacts.
- Timeline. Different types of work have different maturity cycles, and new initiatives have activities that are important to make visible when accounting for your work. 
- Telling the story. Related to both of the above, Logic Models can help connect how quiet, slow, or painstaking work gradually builds towards desired outcomes.
Another characteristic I try to fold into my mentorship programs is to bring mentees further into the peer community, such that they can see how their own issues manifest to varying extents with others. 
To structure these programs, I generally follow this loose structure:
- One: Introductions, development of points of focus
- Two: Logic Models introduction
- Three: They share out their own Logic Model, with special attention to specific topics they wish to focus on
- Four: We introduce an expert peer on one of their chosen issues
- Five: Make sure we're checking in, giving space to vent and share context
- Six: Circle back to one of their primary issues and re-develop that aspect of their Logic Model
